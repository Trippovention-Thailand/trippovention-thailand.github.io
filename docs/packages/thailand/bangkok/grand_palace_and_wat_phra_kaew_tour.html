Tour Overview & Description

Royal Grandeur, Sacred Emerald Buddha & Golden Stupas of Siam's Royal Court

Step into the spiritual and ceremonial heart of the Kingdom of Thailand at the Grand Palace, established in 1782 by King Rama I. Marvel at the dazzling golden chedis, multi-tiered spires, and elaborate mural paintings depicting the Ramakien epic along the royal cloisters.

Within the complex stands Wat Phra Kaew, home to the revered Emerald Buddha (Phra Kaew Morakot), carved from a single block of jadeite. Accompanied by a knowledgeable licensed guide, you will learn the royal rituals, architectural symbolism, and strict customs governing this sacred national sanctuary.

Places Covered & Highlights

Experience the highlights and sights included on this journey

Royal Architecture

Grand Palace Grounds

  • Chakri Maha Prasat Hall: Iconic royal throne hall blending 19th-century European neoclassical architecture with traditional Thai tiered roofs.
  • Dusit Maha Prasat Hall: Exquisite ceremonial hall used for royal coronation ceremonies and lying-in-state services.
  • Royal Cloister Murals: Over 2 kilometers of masterfully restored historic murals illustrating the complete Thai Ramakien epic.
Sacred Landmark

Wat Phra Kaew Sanctuary

  • The Emerald Buddha: Thailand's most venerated Buddhist relic, adorned in seasonal royal gold garments changed by the King.
  • Phra Si Rattana Chedi: Towering gilded stupa enshrining holy relics of the Buddha, glistening in the Bangkok sun.
  • Model of Angkor Wat: Intricate scale model commissioned by King Rama IV celebrating classical Khmer temple architecture.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to common questions about this Bangkok activity

What is the dress code for visiting the Grand Palace?▾

The Grand Palace enforces Thailand's strictest dress code. Both men and women must wear shirts with sleeves covering shoulders, and trousers or long skirts covering ankles. Transparent fabric, sleeveless tops, shorts, ripped jeans, and tight sportswear are strictly prohibited.

Are admission tickets included in the tour price?▾

Yes, official entrance tickets to both the Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew are included in our guided package price with no hidden costs.

Can visitors take photos inside Wat Phra Kaew?▾

Photography is permitted throughout the open palace courtyards and exterior cloisters, but is strictly prohibited inside the main Ubosot chapel where the Emerald Buddha sits.

How early should I book my Grand Palace tour?▾

We recommend reserving at least 48 to 72 hours in advance during peak season (November to April) to ensure licensed guide availability and preferred morning time slots.
