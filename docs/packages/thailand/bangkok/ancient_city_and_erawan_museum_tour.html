Tour Overview & Description

World's Largest Open-Air Museum, Giant Three-Headed Elephant & Recreated Thai Kingdoms

Discover the architectural soul and mythical grandeur of Thailand in one extraordinary day. Located in neighboring Samut Prakan just south of Bangkok, the Ancient City (Muang Boran) is the world's largest open-air museum, spanning over 300 acres shaped precisely like the map of Thailand.

Explore more than 120 scaled and life-sized replicas of Thailand's most revered palaces, sacred sanctuaries, and lost monuments from the Dvaravati, Sukhothai, and Ayutthaya eras. Combine this with the awe-inspiring Erawan Museum, crowned by a monumental 250-ton, 39-meter-tall three-headed bronze elephant housing divine celestial art.

Places Covered & Highlights

Experience the highlights and sights included on this journey

Open-Air Museum

Ancient City (Muang Boran)

  • Sanphet Prasat Palace: Stunning full-scale reconstruction of the lost royal throne hall of Ayutthaya destroyed in 1767.
  • Pavilion of the Enlightened: Spectacular gilded wooden temple rising majestically over tranquil lotus-filled lagoons.
  • Bicycle & Tram Exploration: Enjoy peaceful sightseeing by electric golf cart, open tram, or complimentary bicycle across 300 acres.
Three-Headed Elephant

Erawan Museum

  • Monumental Bronze Elephant: Colossal 39-meter-tall, 250-ton bronze sculpture of Airavata, the mythical three-headed elephant.
  • Stained-Glass Zodiac Ceiling: Breathtaking stained-glass dome created by German master craftsman Jacob Schwarzkopf representing world harmony.
  • Cosmic Heaven Chamber: Sacred celestial shrine located inside the elephant's belly displaying ancient Buddha statues.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to common questions about this Bangkok activity

How do visitors get around the 300-acre Ancient City?▾

Admission includes access to the park's hop-on hop-off tram service and complimentary bicycles. Private 4-seat and 6-seat electric golf carts are also available for rent with a valid driver's license.

Can you go inside the giant three-headed elephant at Erawan Museum?▾

Yes! Visitors can ascend via a spiral staircase or elevator inside one of the elephant's massive legs to reach the top chamber inside the belly, which houses a serene shrine representing Buddhist heaven.

How far are these attractions from Bangkok?▾

Both sites are located in Samut Prakan, just south of Bangkok city limits, approximately 35 to 45 minutes drive from downtown via the Southern Expressway.

Is lunch included in this tour?▾

Lunch is not included in the standard package, but there are multiple authentic Thai food stalls, waterfront restaurants, and a floating market food village inside Ancient City.
